# Mochi and the Moon Bridge

## A storyline for Neon Rush

### The idea in one sentence

Mochi is Chang'e's own rabbit — he belongs to his master, Chang'e, who lives alone in the Guanghan Palace on the Moon. Separated from her, Mochi must run, jump, and slide his way back across three moons to find her — collecting one precious item in every level to rebuild the bridge home.

This is a creative retelling inspired by the Chinese legend of Chang'e, the Moon Goddess, and the Jade Rabbit. In the legend, Chang'e drank an elixir of immortality and floated up to the Moon, where she lives in the Guanghan Palace, and the Jade Rabbit keeps her company, pounding herbs for the elixir of life. *Neon Rush* keeps that heart — a loyal rabbit and a lonely goddess — and turns the journey home into a game.

---

## The characters

**Mochi — the hero, Chang'e's rabbit**
Chang'e's own small, brave moon rabbit — he is hers, and she is his master and family — with soft white fur that glows faintly in the dark. Mochi already appears in *Neon Rush* as a moon rabbit gathering treats across three moons. He is quick, curious, and stubborn in the best way: when he falls, he hops straight back up. He loves mochi treats, but he loves Chang'e more.

**Chang'e — the master**
The Moon Goddess, kind and gentle, living in the Guanghan Palace. She is not a distant queen to be rescued — she is Mochi's family. She sings a soft song every night, and Mochi can hear it faintly whenever the moon is full. She waits, and she believes he will find his way back.

**Wu Gang — the unexpected helper**
The woodcutter of the Moon, doomed to chop a cassia tree that heals itself as fast as he cuts it. He is gruff, tired, and secretly kind. He knows every shortcut on the Moon, and he teaches Mochi that some obstacles are not beaten by force, but by patience and timing.

**The Eclipse Guardian — the final obstacle**
A shadow born from a lunar eclipse, the storm that shattered the Moon Bridge. It is not evil for its own sake — it is lost darkness, jealous of the Moon's light. It guards the palace gate and must be faced, not fled from, in the final Mission level.

## The opening: how Mochi got lost

Once, Mochi lived with his master Chang'e in the Guanghan Palace, where he belonged — pounding sweet herbs beside her and sharing mooncakes under the cinnamon tree. He was her rabbit, and the palace was his home because she was there.

Then came the Night of the Black Eclipse. A shadow storm swept across the sky and struck the Moon Bridge — the shining path that joins the Earth, the three moons, and the palace. The bridge shattered into light and fell like shooting stars.

Mochi was thrown far from home. He woke up in a neon meadow on Earth, dizzy, dusty, and very small — with only one thing left in his pocket: a single silver bell from Chang'e's palace, still warm, still ringing faintly whenever the moon rises.

The bell rings louder when he runs toward the Moon.

So Mochi runs.

---

## The quest: rebuild the Moon Bridge

The bridge cannot be fixed with wood or stone. It is made of moonlight, memory, and kindness, and it needs five lost pieces. Mochi must collect them all, in order, before the palace gate will open. There are six levels, but only the first five award a bridge piece — the sixth is the palace finale, where the pieces are used. Treats (mochi, starlight, sea-glass) are separate: they are score and flavour, not bridge pieces, so a player can never get stuck by missing a treat.

The three moons in the game already have a job to do, so name them in-game: the **Silver Moon**, the **Cinnamon Moon**, and the **Storm Moon**.

1. **The Moon Dew Vial** — captured starlight, to light the way.
2. **The Silver Lantern** — Chang'e's own lantern, to see through darkness.
3. **The Cinnamon Blossom** — a flower from the Moon's cassia tree, to prove a true friend of the palace.
4. **The Tide Pearl** — a pearl from the storm sea, to calm wild waters.
5. **The Eclipse Key** — won from the shadow itself, to unlock the gate.

Only when all five shine together does the bridge sing again.

Each piece is also a gameplay unlock, not just a story token — so the story is felt, not just read:
- **Moon Dew Vial:** lights the launch pad and the first dark stretch.
- **Silver Lantern:** reveals hidden paths and Chang'e's footprints in the dunes.
- **Cinnamon Blossom:** opens the grove gates that Wu Gang guards.
- **Tide Pearl:** calms the storm sea, slowing waves and lightning gates.
- **Eclipse Key:** unlocks the palace gate and weakens the Eclipse Guardian.

And falling is part of the story, not the end of it: when Mochi falls, the silver bell rings, and he restarts at the last checkpoint — the same way he hops straight back up in the legend's spirit. No fall should feel like losing Chang'e.

## The levels

### Level 1 — The Neon Meadow (Earth)
*Where the journey begins.*

Mochi wakes among glowing grass and blinking fireflies, far below the Moon he can see but cannot reach.

- **Obstacles:** rolling neon hay bales, puddle craters, and busy drone-bees that zigzag across the path. Mochi must switch lanes to dodge them, jump the bales, and keep his pace.
- **To collect:** Moon Dew, drop by drop, from glowing flowers — and mochi treats along the way, because a hero runs better on a full stomach.
- **The item won:** **The Moon Dew Vial.** When it is full, a launch pad of light blooms in the meadow, and Mochi leaps — up, up, toward the first moon.
- **The lesson:** *Every great journey starts with one small hop.*

### Level 2 — The Silver Dunes (First Moon)
*A desert of moonlight.*

The first moon is silver sand and deep craters, beautiful and treacherous. The wind whispers here, and sometimes it whispers Mochi's name.

- **Obstacles:** crater pits to jump, falling meteor shards to dodge, and sand serpents that slide across the dunes. Timing matters — jump too early and you land in the pit; too late and the shard catches you.
- **To collect:** scattered starlight and treats buried in the sand.
- **The item won:** **The Silver Lantern.** It belonged to Chang'e, lost in the storm. When Mochi lifts it, the dunes light up, and footprints appear in the sand — Chang'e's footprints, pointing onward.
- **The lesson:** *Even in the dark, someone has walked this way before you.*

### Level 3 — The Cinnamon Grove (Second Moon)
*A forest that grows upside down.*

The second moon is a grove of crystal branches and one enormous cassia tree — the tree Wu Gang has chopped for a thousand years. Its branches heal as fast as they are cut, so the forest is always changing.

- **Obstacles:** swinging crystal branches, thorny vines that block the lanes, and icy slides where Mochi must duck low and slide under falling leaves.
- **To collect:** glowing cinnamon seeds, and treats hidden in hollow branches.
- **The item won:** **The Cinnamon Blossom.** Wu Gang, gruff at first, watches Mochi help a trapped firefly instead of rushing past. Moved, he plucks the blossom himself and hands it over. "Kindness opens more doors than axes," he mutters.
- **The lesson:** *Stopping to help is not losing time — sometimes it is the way forward.*

### Level 4 — The Storm Sea (Third Moon)
*Where the sky meets the water.*

The third moon is an ocean under a storm that never quite ends. Waves rise like walls, lightning draws bright gates across the sky, and whirlpools spin in the lanes.

- **Obstacles:** tidal waves to jump over, lightning gates to time perfectly, and whirlpool lanes that pull Mochi sideways. Here, sliding under low storm arches matters as much as jumping.
- **To collect:** sea-glass, starfish treats, and bubbles of calm air.
- **The item won:** **The Tide Pearl.** Mochi dives — bravely, because rabbits are not supposed to love water — and finds the pearl at the bottom of the calmest whirlpool, guarded by a sleepy moon-turtle who only wakes for a kind word.
- **The lesson:** *Courage is not having no fear. It is hopping anyway.*

### Level 5 — The Shadow Comet (Chase level)
*Run, Mochi, run.*

With four pieces in his satchel, Mochi is chased. The Eclipse Guardian's shadow has taken the shape of a comet and hunts him across the sky. This is the Chase level: there is no time to stop and look around.

- **Obstacles:** everything at once — comet debris, collapsing star bridges, and shadow hands reaching from the dark. Mochi must switch lanes, jump, and slide in rhythm, never breaking stride.
- **To collect:** shards of light, snatched mid-run, each one weakening the shadow a little.
- **The item won:** **The Eclipse Key.** At the end of the chase, the shadow corners Mochi — and Mochi, instead of running, turns and offers it a mochi treat. The shadow, which has never been offered anything, falters. The key falls from its grasp.
- **The lesson:** *You cannot outrun every darkness. Sometimes you have to turn and face it — kindly.*

### Level 6 — The Guanghan Palace (Mission finale)
*Home, at last.*

The palace gate is made of moonlight and locked with five lights. Mochi places the Moon Dew Vial, the Silver Lantern, the Cinnamon Blossom, the Tide Pearl, and the Eclipse Key into the gate. One by one, they shine. The Moon Bridge sings — and rebuilds itself, arching across the sky.

But the Eclipse Guardian makes one last stand. This is the Mission guardian fight: Mochi must dodge its shadow bolts, time his jumps, and fire back beams of moonlight.

- **Obstacles:** shadow bolts, eclipse rings that shrink the safe ground, and a final storm of falling stars.
- **To collect:** the last mochi treats, scattered by the battle, and the courage Mochi has gathered all along.
- **The final item:** **The Mooncake of Reunion.** Chang'e herself comes down the palace steps, holding it. She does not ask Mochi to be perfect, or fast, or fearless. She just opens her arms.
- **The ending:** Mochi and Chang'e share the mooncake under the cinnamon tree, while Wu Gang — finally, for one night — puts down his axe and joins them. The next morning, the cassia tree he has chopped for a thousand years blooms once, in honour of a rabbit who kept going — a small sign that even an endless task can change. The bridge stays open now, so Mochi can visit Earth and return whenever he wishes. Home, it turns out, was never just a place. It was the people waiting for you at the end of the run.

## Lines the game can actually use

Short, so they fit a HUD or overlay. Keep Mochi's voice small and determined, Chang'e's warm and unhurried:

- **Level 1 start, Mochi:** "Bell's ringing louder. That way — toward the Moon."
- **Level 2, on finding the lantern:** "Her footprints. I'm on the right path."
- **Level 3, Wu Gang:** "Kindness opens more doors than axes. Take the blossom."
- **Level 4, the turtle:** "A kind word wakes a friend. A kind word calms a sea."
- **Level 5, Mochi to the shadow:** "You don't have to be dark alone. Have a mochi."
- **Level 6, Chang'e:** "You came all this way. Sit. Share this mooncake with me."

## How the story maps to the game

| Story moment | Gameplay |
|---|---|
| Dodging bales, bees, and meteor shards | Switch lanes (Arrow keys / A/D), jump (Space) |
| Sliding under vines, leaves, and storm arches | Slide (Down, in Chase and Mission) |
| Facing the Eclipse Guardian | Fire moonlight (F, during the Mission guardian fight) |
| The Shadow Comet chase | Chase mode — keep pace, never stop |
| Collecting treats and the five bridge pieces | Pickups in every level; all five are needed to open the palace gate |
| Pausing to help the firefly, the turtle, the shadow | The story's heart: kindness is a mechanic, not just a moral |

---

## Why this story fits Neon Rush

- It keeps what the game already has: Mochi, the moon rabbit, gathering treats across three moons — and gives that gathering a reason.
- It turns the game's real mechanics (lane switching, jumping, sliding, chasing, and the guardian fight) into story beats, instead of bolting a story on top of them.
- It honours the Chang'e legend without copying it flatly: the palace, the cinnamon tree, Wu Gang, and the Jade Rabbit's kindness are all there, retold as a small hero's big run home.
- And it ends the way a runner should end: not with the hero becoming a god, but with a shared mooncake, a rebuilt bridge, and a friend waiting at the door.
